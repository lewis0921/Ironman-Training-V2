Pacing and fuelling

  • Bike at Ironman power from the first hour and never above it. The first 20 minutes stay below it while your heart rate settles.
  • Run the first 5 km at the slow end of the range even though it feels too easy.
  • Carbohydrate 80–90 g per hour on the bike from 10 minutes in, then 50–70 g per hour on the run. Use the products you train with.

Taper

Week 46 drops to about three quarters of peak volume, week 47 to about half, and race week to about a third. Short race-pace pieces stay in.

Method

Built from general principles associated with these coaches, not from their published plans.

  • Dan Lorang: repeating cycles of skill and VO2max, then strength-endurance, then race-pace work. Slow, consistent progression.
  • Dan Plews: mostly easy work early, more time between the thresholds as the race nears (polarised to pyramidal). Low-cadence strength intervals. Readiness check by heart-rate variability.
  • Lance Watson: long rides finishing with race-pace bricks, race fuelling practised on every long day, and never racing harder than you trained.
  • Blummenfelt and Bu: lots of easy volume plus controlled sub-threshold intervals with short rests, using power and pace ceilings in place of lactate testing.

Readiness rule

If your HRV is below your normal range for two days and you feel flat, swap that day's key session for easy endurance at the same length. Don't make it up later.

Recovery and benchmarks

Every 4th week up to week 42 is a recovery week with about 30% less volume and no intervals. Re-test FTP, run threshold and swim CSS in those weeks, then update the Your numbers tab.
